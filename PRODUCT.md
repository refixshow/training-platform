# Product

## Register

product

## Users

The product serves two primary user groups: coaches and people being coached. Trainees use it in a practical training context, often on mobile, to understand assigned work, fill in training results, track progress, and upload progress photos without friction. Coaches use it as a more detailed operating surface for building exercises, routines, programs, reviewing submitted training summaries, tracking activity, and interpreting client progress over time.

## Product Purpose

The application helps coaches manage structured strength and fitness coaching. It supports an exercise library with media, exercise types, equipment, muscle groups, instructions, and videos; routines composed of typed exercise sets; multi-week programs; user assignments; training submissions; progress photos; and statistics such as weekly duration, volume, sets, bodyweight, progress photo sliders, and activity maps.

Success means trainees can complete their assigned training flow with minimal confusion, while coaches can see enough detail to make programming decisions, review adherence, and understand progress across clients.

## Brand Personality

Clear, practical, professional. The interface should feel focused and trustworthy, with a calm operational tone rather than loud motivation. It should make training feel structured and manageable for the trainee, while giving coaches the depth and control expected from a serious coaching tool.

## Anti-references

Avoid copying Heavycoach branding or visual identity directly. Avoid fitness influencer aesthetics, bodybuilding kitsch, motivational clutter, overloaded dashboards, generic SaaS pastel styling, and dark interfaces that make mobile training input harder to read. Avoid hiding important coaching detail behind overly simplified UI.

## Design Principles

1. Trainee first at the point of action: logging a workout, reading instructions, and uploading progress should be fast, legible, and forgiving.
2. Coach depth without dashboard noise: detailed programming and progress data should be available through clear hierarchy, filters, and progressive disclosure.
3. Every training metric needs context: volume, sets, duration, activity, bodyweight, and photos should help explain progress, not just decorate the interface.
4. Media supports confidence: exercise photos, instructions, and videos should reduce uncertainty before a trainee starts a set.
5. Structure beats motivation: the product should feel dependable and serious, using clear workflows instead of hype language.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Aim for WCAG AA contrast and interaction standards. Prioritize mobile-first readability for trainees using the app around training sessions. Support reduced motion for non-essential animation. Avoid relying on color alone for status, intensity, completion, or warning states. Use clear labels for numeric training inputs such as weight, reps, RPE, rest duration, distance, bodyweight, and duration.
